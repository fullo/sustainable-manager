# CBAM Product Scope Reference

Comprehensive reference for products covered by the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (Regulation (EU) 2023/956, as amended by the CBAM Omnibus Regulation (EU) 2025/2083), including CN codes, emission types, calculation methods, exemptions, and key deadlines. Last verified: July 2026.

---

## 1. Products Covered by Sector

### 1.1 Cement (Annex I, Section 1)

| CN Code | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 2523 10 00 | Cement clinker |
| 2523 21 00 | White Portland cement, whether or not artificially coloured |
| 2523 29 00 | Other Portland cement |
| 2523 30 00 | Aluminous cement |
| 2523 90 00 | Other hydraulic cements |

**Emission profile:**
- Direct emissions: calcination of limestone (CaCO3 -> CaO + CO2), fuel combustion in kilns
- Indirect emissions: electricity consumed in grinding, material handling, kiln operation auxiliaries
- Key precursor: cement clinker (CN 2523 10 00)
- Typical emission intensity: 0.6-0.9 tCO2/t cement (varies by clinker ratio and fuel mix)

**Calculation notes:**
- Clinker-to-cement ratio is the primary driver of direct emissions
- Alternative fuels (waste-derived, biomass) reduce net fossil CO2
- Supplementary cementitious materials (fly ash, slag, pozzolana) reduce clinker ratio

**Calculation notes:**
- Production route identification is critical (BF-BOF vs EAF vs DRI)
- Scrap ratio in EAF significantly affects emissions
- For downstream products (Chapter 73), embedded emissions include precursor steel emissions
- Complex goods may use multiple steel precursors

### 1.3 Aluminium (Annex I, Section 3)

| CN Code Range | Description |
|---------------|-------------|
| 7601 | Unwrought aluminium |
| 7602 | Aluminium waste and scrap |
| 7603 | Aluminium powders and flakes |
| 7604 | Aluminium bars, rods and profiles |
| 7605 | Aluminium wire |
| 7606 | Aluminium plates, sheets and strip, thickness > 0.2mm |
| 7607 | Aluminium foil, thickness <= 0.2mm |
| 7608 | Aluminium tubes and pipes |
| 7609 | Aluminium tube or pipe fittings |
| 7610 | Aluminium structures and parts of structures |
| 7611 | Aluminium reservoirs, tanks, vats and similar containers, capacity > 300 litres |
| 7612 | Aluminium casks, drums, cans, boxes and similar containers, capacity <= 300 litres |
| 7613 | Aluminium containers for compressed or liquefied gas |
| 7614 | Stranded wire, cables, plaited bands and the like, of aluminium, not electrically insulated |
| 7616 | Other articles of aluminium |

**Emission profile:**
- Direct emissions: smelting (Hall-Heroult electrolysis process), anode effects (PFC emissions), anode baking
  - Primary aluminium: ~1.5-2.0 tCO2/t (direct only)
- Indirect emissions: extremely electricity-intensive (~13-16 MWh/t aluminium)
  - Can range from ~1 tCO2/t (hydropower) to ~15 tCO2/t (coal-based grid)
  - Indirect emissions often exceed direct emissions
- Key precursor: unwrought aluminium (7601)

**Calculation notes:**
- Electricity source is the dominant factor in total emissions
- Secondary (recycled) aluminium has significantly lower emissions (~0.3-0.5 tCO2/t)
- Distinction between primary and secondary production is critical
- Anode type matters: pre-baked vs Soderberg anodes have different PFC emission profiles

**Emission profile:**
- Direct emissions:

  - Nitric acid production: N2O emissions (~0.5-3.0 tCO2e/t HNO3 depending on abatement)
  - Urea production: CO2 used as feedstock but partially released during application
- Indirect emissions: electricity for compression, air separation, granulation
- Key precursors: ammonia (2814), nitric acid (2808 00 00)

**Calculation notes:**
- Ammonia is the key precursor for most nitrogen fertilizers
- N2O abatement technology in nitric acid production is a major differentiator
- Mixed fertilizers (3105) require allocation of emissions across nutrient components
- Urea has a unique carbon balance: CO2 is incorporated during production but released during use

### 1.5 Hydrogen (Annex I, Section 5)

| CN Code | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 2804 10 00 | Hydrogen |

**Emission profile:**
- Direct emissions: depend entirely on production method
- Indirect emissions: electricity consumption, particularly relevant for electrolysis

**Production method classification:**

| Color | Method | Direct Emissions | Indirect Emissions |
|-------|--------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Grey | Steam Methane Reforming (SMR) | ~9-12 tCO2/t H2 | Low |
| Blue | SMR + Carbon Capture (CCS) | ~1-4 tCO2/t H2 | Low |
| Green | Electrolysis (renewable electricity) | ~0 tCO2/t H2 | ~0 tCO2/t H2 |
| Turquoise | Methane pyrolysis | ~0-2 tCO2/t H2 | Low-Medium |
| Pink/Purple | Electrolysis (nuclear electricity) | ~0 tCO2/t H2 | Low |
| Yellow | Electrolysis (grid electricity) | ~0 tCO2/t H2 | Varies by grid |

**Calculation notes:**
- Production method must be verified through producer documentation
- For SMR: methane slip and upstream emissions may be significant
- For electrolysis: electricity source and grid emission factor are critical
- CCS capture rate (typically 85-95%) determines residual emissions for blue hydrogen
- Hydrogen is increasingly traded; origin and method certification becoming standardized

### 1.6 Electricity (Annex I, Section 6)

| CN Code | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| 2716 00 00 | Electrical energy |

**Emission profile:**
- Direct emissions only (indirect emissions not applicable)
- Based on the emission factor of the exporting country's grid or specific generation source
- Specific rules for electricity imported via interconnectors

**Calculation methods (specific to electricity):**
1. **Default value**: CO2 emission factor of the exporting country's grid (published by EC)
2. **Specific value**: Where the importer can demonstrate a direct contract with a specific generator and physical delivery via an interconnector (strict conditions apply)

**Calculation notes:**

## 2. Calculation Methods

### 2.1 Actual Emissions (Preferred Method)

Requirements for using actual installation-level data:
- Data must come from the production installation where the goods were produced
- Must cover a defined reporting period (calendar year or compliance period)
- Must include all relevant emission sources within the installation boundary
- Must use recognized monitoring methodologies (comparable to EU ETS MRR)
- Should be verified by an independent accredited verifier (mandatory from definitive phase)

Data elements required from producer:
- Installation identification and location
- Production processes and routes
- Fuel types and quantities consumed
- Raw material inputs and chemical transformations
- Electricity consumed (source and quantity)
- Total production output
- Specific embedded emissions per tonne of product

### 2.2 EU Default Values

Published by the European Commission for each product category. Based on:
- Average emission intensity of the worst-performing 10% of EU installations
- Updated periodically
- Available in the CBAM transitional registry and implementing regulation annexes

When to use:
- Producer does not provide data
- Data quality is insufficient
- As a conservative fallback during transitional period

### 2.3 Country Default Values

Available where:
- The third country has a reliable national inventory or emissions monitoring system
- Data has been assessed and recognized by the European Commission
- Covers the specific product category

Countries with recognized default values are published by the EC and updated over time.

### 2.4 Fallback Values

When no other method is available:
- EU default values apply with an additional markup
- Represents the most conservative (highest) emission estimate
- Designed to incentivize producers to provide actual data

## 3. De Minimis Threshold and Exemptions

### 3.1 De Minimis (50 Tonnes) — as amended by Reg. (EU) 2025/2083

- Single mass-based threshold: applies to the **cumulative net mass of all CBAM goods** (cement, iron & steel, aluminium, fertilizers) imported by the importer in the calendar year — NOT per product type
- **Hydrogen and electricity are excluded** from the de minimis exemption
- If cumulative imports stay at or below 50 tonnes in a calendar year, CBAM obligations do not apply
- The threshold is based on net mass of goods, not embedded emissions
- Must be assessed aggregating all imports across the year (not per shipment); importers approaching the threshold should monitor volumes to avoid inadvertent non-compliance
- Commission estimate: exempts ~90% of importers while ~99% of embedded emissions remain covered

### 3.2 Country Exemptions

CBAM does not apply to goods originating from:
- **EU Member States** (intra-EU trade)
- **EEA/EFTA countries** (Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway) where linked to EU ETS
- **Switzerland** (linked ETS)
- Countries or territories listed in Annex III, Section 2 of Regulation (EU) 2023/956, provided they have an ETS linked to or equivalent to the EU ETS

### 3.3 Other Exemptions

- **Returned goods**: Goods that are returned to the EU after being previously exported, under certain customs conditions
- **Military goods**: Goods imported for military use under specific provisions
- **Personal effects**: Below the customs duty threshold (generally not relevant for CBAM product categories)
- **Goods in transit**: Goods merely transiting through the EU customs territory

## 4. Key Deadlines and Phases

### 4.1 Transitional Phase (October 1, 2023 - December 31, 2025)

| Deadline | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| End of month after each quarter | Submit quarterly CBAM report to competent authority |
| Q4 2023 report | Due January 31, 2024 (first report) |
| Q1-Q4 2024 reports | Due April 30, July 31, October 31, 2024, and January 31, 2025 |
| Q1-Q4 2025 reports | Due April 30, July 31, October 31, 2025, and January 31, 2026 |

Transitional phase characteristics:
- Reporting obligations only (no financial cost)
- Use of estimated values permitted with certain tolerances
- No need for authorized declarant status (reporting by importer or customs representative)
- Use of the CBAM Transitional Registry (European Commission IT platform)

### 4.2 Definitive Phase (January 1, 2026 onwards) — as amended by Reg. (EU) 2025/2083

| Deadline | Requirement |
|----------|-------------|
| March 31, 2026 | Importers who applied for authorized CBAM declarant status by this date may continue importing while the application is pending |
| February 1, 2027 | CBAM certificate sales start on the EU central platform; certificates for 2026 imports purchased retroactively at the quarterly average EU ETS price of the import period |
| End of each quarter | Hold certificates covering at least **50%** of embedded emissions of goods imported since the start of the year (reduced from 80%) |
| By September 30 each year | Submit annual CBAM declaration AND surrender certificates for previous calendar year (declaration deadline moved from May 31) |
| After September 30 | Request repurchase of excess certificates (up to 1/3 of total purchased in preceding year) |

First definitive-phase cycle:
- 2026: First year of CBAM financial obligations (obligation accrues; no certificate purchase yet)
- February 1, 2027: Certificate sales open
- September 30, 2027: First annual CBAM declaration and certificate surrender (for 2026 imports)

## 5. Carbon Price Credit

### 5.1 Eligible Carbon Prices

A carbon price effectively paid in the country of origin can be deducted from CBAM certificate obligations. Eligible instruments include:
- Emissions trading systems (cap-and-trade)
- Carbon taxes
- Carbon levies or fees
- Other mandatory carbon pricing mechanisms

### 5.2 Documentation Requirements

To claim a carbon price credit, the authorized declarant must provide:
- Evidence of the carbon pricing instrument in the origin country
- Proof of payment by the producer or on behalf of the producer
- The effective price paid per tCO2e
- Confirmation that no rebate, compensation, or free allocation was received that would offset the carbon price
- Currency conversion at the applicable exchange rate

### 5.3 Calculation

```
Carbon price credit (EUR/tCO2e) = Carbon price paid (local currency) / Exchange rate - Any rebates or free allocations received
```

The credit is applied per tCO2e of embedded emissions, reducing the number of CBAM certificates to be surrendered.
