# Socratic Interview Guide for Sustainability Assessment

This guide structures the consulting conversation when a user needs to build a sustainability report from scratch or assess their company's sustainability posture. The approach is Socratic: ask one question at a time, explain why it matters, help the user think through their answer, and adapt the flow based on what you learn.

## Principles

- **One question at a time.** Don't overwhelm with a questionnaire. Each question builds on the previous answer.
- **Explain the "why."** Before asking, briefly explain why the information matters (regulatory requirement, materiality driver, stakeholder expectation).
- **Offer examples.** When asking about metrics or policies, give concrete examples of what good answers look like so the user understands the level of detail needed.
- **Be adaptive.** If the user reveals they're in a specific sector, tailor subsequent questions to that sector's material topics. If they already have certain certifications, skip what's already covered.
- **Summarize periodically.** Every 4-5 questions, recap what you've gathered so the user can correct or expand.
- **Flag quick wins.** If you spot easy improvements during the interview, note them — but stay focused on data gathering first.

## Italian Context & Benchmarks

When working with Italian companies, useful reference points:
- **BDO Italy (2025)**: 94% of Italian companies maintained or increased sustainability budgets in 2025, above the European average
- **CRIF ESG Outlook 2025**: 77.6% of CSRD-compliant Italian companies had ESG ratings of "Very High" or "High", vs. only 46% among those without non-financial reporting
- **CSRD scope in Italy**: ~200 companies under NFRD → ~5,000 under CSRD
- **Key Italian resources**: Confindustria (regional chapters), ISMEA/Federalimentare (food sector), Unioncamere, Assolombarda sustainability desks
- **Italian transposition**: D.Lgs. that transposes Directive 2022/2464 — check for national-specific requirements
- **Academic integration**: Major Italian universities (Bocconi, Alma Mater Bologna, Politecnico di Milano) have integrated ESG into curricula — users may be students or thesis writers

Use these benchmarks to contextualize: "Your energy intensity of X MWh/employee is [above/below/in line with] Italian manufacturing averages." This makes the analysis actionable rather than abstract.

## Phase 1: Company Profile

Start here. This determines which frameworks apply and which topics are likely material.

**Questions to explore (adapt based on answers):**

1. What does your company do? What sector/industry are you in?
   - *Why: Sector determines material topics and may trigger sector-specific standards (GRI Sector Standards, SASB industry standards)*

2. How large is your company? (Employees, revenue, total assets)
   - *Why: Determines CSRD applicability thresholds and reporting timeline*

3. Where are you headquartered and where do you operate?
   - *Why: Geography determines regulatory obligations (EU = CSRD), and operational locations affect environmental/social impacts*

4. Describe your value chain briefly — who are your main suppliers, and who are your customers?
   - *Why: ESRS requires value chain consideration. Scope 3 emissions and supply chain due diligence depend on this*

5. Are you publicly listed? On which exchange?
   - *Why: Listed companies have additional disclosure requirements and earlier CSRD deadlines*

**After this phase**, you should know:
- CSRD applicability and timeline
- Likely material sectors for SASB/GRI
- Geographic scope of impacts
- Value chain complexity

## Phase 2: Current State Assessment

Understand what the company already does before identifying gaps.

**Questions to explore:**

1. Have you published a sustainability report before? Under which framework?
   - *Why: Existing reports are a goldmine — they show what data you already collect and how mature your processes are*

2. What sustainability data do you currently track? (Emissions, energy, water, waste, HR metrics...)
   - *Why: Identifies what's available vs. what needs new data collection processes*

3. Do you have any environmental certifications? (ISO 14001, EMAS, B Corp, sector-specific...)
   - *Why: Certifications often mean management systems and data are already in place*

4. Do you have sustainability-related policies? (Environmental policy, human rights policy, anti-corruption, diversity...)
   - *Why: ESRS requires disclosure of policies for each material topic (MDR-P)*

5. Is there a dedicated sustainability role or team? Who has board-level oversight?
   - *Why: ESRS 2 GOV-1 requires disclosure of governance structure for sustainability matters*

6. Have you done a materiality assessment before?
   - *Why: This is the foundation of ESRS reporting — if they've done one, it shapes everything that follows*

**After this phase**, you should know:
- Reporting maturity level (first-time vs. experienced)
- Available data and gaps
- Existing management systems and policies
- Governance readiness

## Phase 3: Materiality Assessment

This is the heart of the process. Guide the user through identifying their material topics using the double materiality lens.

**Approach:**

1. Based on the sector identified in Phase 1, present the likely material topics:
   - "For companies in [sector], the typical material topics are: [list]. Do these resonate with your situation?"

2. For each candidate topic, explore both materiality dimensions:
   - **Impact materiality**: "Does your company have significant positive or negative impacts related to [topic]? On whom?"
   - **Financial materiality**: "Could [topic] create financial risks or opportunities for your company? How?"

3. Help them prioritize using a scale:
   - Impact: severity (scale, scope, irremediability) and likelihood
   - Financial: magnitude of potential financial effect and likelihood

4. Map material topics to ESRS standards and specific disclosures required

**Key questions per topic area:**

*Climate/Energy (ESRS E1):*
- What are your main sources of energy? Do you track consumption?
- Do you know your Scope 1 and Scope 2 GHG emissions?
- Have you considered Scope 3 (supply chain, product use, business travel)?
- Do you have reduction targets? Are they science-based?

*Pollution (ESRS E2):*
- Does your company emit pollutants to air, water, or soil?
- Do you handle substances of concern (REACH, SVHC)?

*Water (ESRS E3):*
- Is water significant for your operations or supply chain?
- Do you operate in water-stressed areas?

*Biodiversity (ESRS E4):*
- Do you operate near protected areas or sensitive ecosystems?
- Does your supply chain involve land use change?

*Circular Economy (ESRS E5):*
- What are your main material inputs? Are any from recycled sources?
- What waste do you generate? What's the recycling/recovery rate?

*Own Workforce (ESRS S1):*
- How many employees? Full-time vs. part-time, permanent vs. temporary?
- What's your gender pay gap? Diversity at management level?
- Health and safety: incident rates, fatalities?
- Training hours per employee?

*Value Chain Workers (ESRS S2):*
- Do you have visibility into working conditions at key suppliers?
- Have you conducted human rights due diligence?
- Do you have a supplier code of conduct? Is it enforced?

*Sustainable Procurement (ISO 20400):*
- Do you have a sustainable procurement policy?
- Do your procurement processes include sustainability criteria alongside price and quality?
- How do you evaluate supplier sustainability performance? (certifications, audits, self-assessments, data?)
- Do you apply lifecycle costing (total cost of ownership) or only compare purchase prices?
- Do you invest in supplier development — helping key suppliers improve their sustainability practices?
- *Why this matters: ESRS S2 requires due diligence on value chain workers, ESRS E1 Scope 3 emissions are driven by purchased goods, and ESRS G1 covers payment practices. ISO 20400 provides the operational framework to deliver on all of these. Procurement is where sustainability strategy meets real-world supply chain impact.*

*Communities (ESRS S3):*
- Do your operations affect local communities? How?
- Do you engage with community stakeholders?

*Business Conduct (ESRS G1):*
- Do you have anti-corruption and anti-bribery policies?
- What about whistleblowing mechanisms?
- Payment practices with suppliers?

## Phase 4: Data Collection

For each material topic identified, gather the specific metrics needed.

**Approach:**

1. Present the required disclosures for each material topic
2. For each metric, ask:
   - "Do you have this data? For which years?"
   - "What's the source/methodology?"
   - "Are there any limitations or estimates?"
3. Help them understand units and boundaries:
   - GHG emissions: tCO2e, by scope, by activity
   - Energy: MWh or GJ, by source (renewable vs. non-renewable)
   - Water: m3, by source and destination
   - Workforce: headcount vs. FTE, as of what date
4. If they don't have the data, help them plan how to collect it

## Phase 5: Targets & Strategy

**Questions to explore:**

1. Do you have quantitative sustainability targets? (Emission reduction, renewable energy, waste reduction, diversity...)
   - *Why: ESRS requires targets for each material topic (MDR-T)*

2. Are your climate targets science-based? Have you committed to SBTi?
   - *Why: ESRS E1 specifically asks about alignment with the Paris Agreement*

3. Do you have a climate transition plan?
   - *Why: ESRS E1 requires disclosure of the transition plan for climate change mitigation*

4. What actions are you taking or planning to achieve your targets?
   - *Why: ESRS requires disclosure of actions (MDR-A) with timelines and expected outcomes*

5. How is sustainability integrated into your business strategy?
   - *Why: ESRS 2 SBM-1 requires description of strategy and business model*

## Phase 6: Synthesis & Output

After gathering sufficient information:

1. **Summarize findings** in a structured format
2. **Gap analysis**: What's missing for ESRS compliance? Prioritize by:
   - Regulatory urgency (mandatory disclosures first)
   - Data availability (quick wins vs. long-term data collection needs)
   - Materiality (focus on most material topics first)
3. **Offer next steps**:
   - Generate visualizations of collected data
   - Draft report sections
   - Create an action plan with timeline
   - Recommend data collection improvements

## Handling Incomplete Information

The user will rarely have everything. That's OK.

- Accept estimates and ranges when exact data isn't available
- Note data gaps explicitly — they become part of the improvement roadmap
- Prioritize: some disclosures are mandatory regardless of materiality (ESRS 2), others depend on the materiality assessment
- Suggest pragmatic approaches: "You don't need perfect data to start. A reasonable estimate with a clear methodology is better than no disclosure."
