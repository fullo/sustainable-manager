## Historical Context: NFRD → CSRD

The **Non-Financial Reporting Directive (NFRD, 2014/95/EU)** was the first EU directive requiring large public-interest entities (>500 employees) to disclose non-financial information on environmental, social, and governance matters. In Italy, it was transposed through D.Lgs. 254/2016, applying to approximately 200 companies.

**Key limitations of NFRD** that led to CSRD:
- Limited scope (~11,700 companies across the EU, ~200 in Italy)
- No mandatory reporting standards — companies could choose their own framework
- No assurance requirement
- Inconsistent quality and comparability across reports
- No digital taxonomy for machine-readable data

The **CSRD (Directive 2022/2464)** replaced and vastly expanded the NFRD, bringing the number of obligated companies to ~50,000 across the EU (~5,000 in Italy). It introduced mandatory standards (ESRS), digital tagging (XBRL), and external assurance requirements.

## ESRS / CSRD

The **European Sustainability Reporting Standards (ESRS)** are the mandatory reporting standards under the **Corporate Sustainability Reporting Directive (CSRD)**.

### Applicability
- Large EU companies (>250 employees or >40M revenue or >20M total assets — 2 of 3)
- Listed SMEs (with transitional provisions)
- Non-EU companies with significant EU operations (>150M EU revenue)
- Phased rollout: large companies from FY2024, listed SMEs from FY2026

### The 12 ESRS Standards

**Cross-cutting:**
- **ESRS 1** — General requirements (materiality assessment methodology, value chain boundaries)
- **ESRS 2** — General disclosures (governance, strategy, impact/risk/opportunity management, metrics & targets)

**Environmental (E):**
- **ESRS E1** — Climate change (GHG emissions Scope 1/2/3, transition plans, carbon pricing)
- **ESRS E2** — Pollution (air, water, soil pollutants, substances of concern)
- **ESRS E3** — Water and marine resources (consumption, withdrawals, discharges)
- **ESRS E4** — Biodiversity and ecosystems (impacts on habitats, species, ecosystem services)
- **ESRS E5** — Resource use and circular economy (material inflows/outflows, waste, recycling)

**Social (S):**
- **ESRS S1** — Own workforce (working conditions, equal treatment, health & safety, training)
- **ESRS S2** — Workers in the value chain (due diligence, engagement, remediation)
- **ESRS S3** — Affected communities (human rights impacts, engagement processes)
- **ESRS S4** — Consumers and end-users (product safety, data privacy, accessibility)

**Governance (G):**
- **ESRS G1** — Business conduct (anti-corruption, lobbying, payment practices, whistleblowing)

### Double Materiality
ESRS requires **double materiality assessment**:
- **Impact materiality**: How the company affects people and environment (inside-out)
- **Financial materiality**: How sustainability matters affect the company financially (outside-in)

A topic is material if it meets EITHER threshold. Each material topic then triggers specific disclosure requirements.

### Key Disclosure Types
- **Policies** (MDR-P): Describe the policy addressing the topic
- **Actions** (MDR-A): Specific actions taken or planned
- **Metrics** (MDR-M): Quantitative measurements
- **Targets** (MDR-T): Time-bound targets with base year and milestones

## GRI Standards

The **Global Reporting Initiative** standards are the most widely used sustainability reporting framework globally.

### Structure (2021 revision)
- **GRI 1** — Foundation (principles, requirements for "in accordance" reporting)
- **GRI 2** — General Disclosures (organization profile, governance, strategy, stakeholder engagement)
- **GRI 3** — Material Topics (process to determine material topics)
- **Sector Standards** (GRI 11-16+): Sector-specific material topics (Oil & Gas, Coal, Agriculture, Mining, Textiles...)
- **Topic Standards** (GRI 200/300/400 series): Specific disclosures per topic

### Key Topic Standards
**Economic (200 series):**
- GRI 201: Economic Performance
- GRI 205: Anti-corruption
- GRI 207: Tax

**Environmental (300 series):**
- GRI 302: Energy
- GRI 303: Water and Effluents
- GRI 305: Emissions (305-1 Scope 1, 305-2 Scope 2, 305-3 Scope 3)
- GRI 306: Waste

**New climate/energy topic standards (effective 1 January 2027):**
- **GRI 102: Climate Change 2025** — supersedes climate content of GRI 305 for climate impacts (transition plans, adaptation, just transition); designed to be complementary to IFRS S2
- **GRI 103: Energy 2025** — supersedes GRI 302
- Reports covering FY2026 published in 2027 must apply them; early adoption possible

**Social (400 series):**
- GRI 401: Employment
- GRI 403: Occupational Health and Safety
- GRI 404: Training and Education
- GRI 405: Diversity and Equal Opportunity
- GRI 413: Local Communities

### GRI + ESRS Interoperability
EFRAG and GRI have published an interoperability mapping. Many ESRS disclosures correspond directly to GRI topic standards. Companies already reporting under GRI have a head start on ESRS compliance.

## SASB / ISSB

**SASB** (Sustainability Accounting Standards Board) provides industry-specific standards focused on financially material sustainability topics. Now consolidated under the **ISSB** (International Sustainability Standards Board) as part of **IFRS S1** and **IFRS S2**.

### Key Characteristics
- **77 industry standards** across 11 sectors
- Focus on **financial materiality** (investor-oriented)
- Metrics are quantitative and comparable within industries
- IFRS S1 (General sustainability disclosures) and IFRS S2 (Climate) build on SASB + TCFD
- **Adoption status (April 2026)**: 28 jurisdictions have adopted or introduced ISSB standards (voluntary or mandatory), with a further ~12 planning to; IFRS S2 received targeted amendments in December 2025
- **Nature next**: an ISSB exposure draft on nature-related disclosures is targeted for CBD COP17 (October 2026), building on TNFD

### SASB's Five Dimensions
1. Environment
2. Social Capital
3. Human Capital
4. Business Model & Innovation
5. Leadership & Governance

### EU Context
ISSB/IFRS sustainability standards are being considered for adoption or endorsement in the EU as a complement to ESRS. ESRS covers a broader scope (double materiality) while ISSB focuses on investor-relevant financial materiality.

## TCFD

The **Task Force on Climate-related Financial Disclosures** provides a framework specifically for climate risk and opportunity reporting.

### Four Pillars

1. **Governance**: Board oversight and management's role in climate matters
2. **Strategy**: Climate risks/opportunities, scenario analysis, resilience
3. **Risk Management**: Processes for identifying, assessing, managing climate risks
4. **Metrics and Targets**: GHG emissions, climate-related metrics, reduction targets

### Scenario Analysis
TCFD recommends scenario analysis using at least two scenarios:
- A **well-below 2C** scenario (orderly transition)
- A **high warming** scenario (4C+, physical risks dominate)

### Integration with ESRS
ESRS E1 (Climate change) incorporates TCFD recommendations. Companies meeting ESRS E1 requirements largely satisfy TCFD as well. The transition plan requirements in ESRS go beyond TCFD.

## UN SDGs

The **17 Sustainable Development Goals** provide a universal framework for sustainable development.

### The 17 Goals
1. No Poverty
2. Zero Hunger
3. Good Health and Well-being
4. Quality Education
5. Gender Equality
6. Clean Water and Sanitation
7. Affordable and Clean Energy
8. Decent Work and Economic Growth
9. Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure
10. Reduced Inequalities
11. Sustainable Cities and Communities
12. Responsible Consumption and Production
13. Climate Action
14. Life Below Water
15. Life on Land
16. Peace, Justice and Strong Institutions
17. Partnerships for the Goals

### How to Use SDGs in Reporting
SDGs are not a reporting standard per se, but are useful for:
- **Stakeholder communication**: Framing sustainability efforts in universally understood terms
- **Impact mapping**: Linking company activities to broader societal outcomes
- **Strategy alignment**: Identifying which SDGs the company most contributes to (positively or negatively)
- **Target setting**: Aligning company targets with national/global SDG targets

### SDG-Framework Mapping
Most material ESRS/GRI topics can be mapped to relevant SDGs:
- ESRS E1 / GRI 305 → SDG 13 (Climate Action)
- ESRS E3 / GRI 303 → SDG 6 (Clean Water), SDG 14 (Life Below Water)
- ESRS S1 / GRI 401,403 → SDG 8 (Decent Work), SDG 3 (Health)
- ESRS G1 / GRI 205 → SDG 16 (Peace, Justice)

## ISO 20400 Sustainable Procurement

**ISO 20400:2017** provides guidance for integrating sustainability into procurement processes. Not certifiable, but foundational for supply chain sustainability.

For detailed guidance, read `references/procurement.md`. Key points:

### Seven Principles (from ISO 26000)
1. Organizational Governance
2. Human Rights
3. Labor Practices
4. Environment
5. Fair Operating Practices
6. Consumer Issues
7. Community Involvement

### Five Procurement Phases
1. **Planning** — sustainability risk assessment per category
2. **Sourcing** — sustainability criteria in tenders
3. **Evaluation & Award** — lifecycle costing, not just price
4. **Contract Management** — sustainability KPIs in contracts
5. **Supplier Development** — capability building, collaboration

### Connection to ESRS
- ESRS S2 (Value Chain Workers) ← ISO 20400 Human Rights + Labor Practices
- ESRS E1 Scope 3 ← ISO 20400 Environmental criteria in procurement
- ESRS G1 (Business Conduct) ← ISO 20400 Fair Operating Practices + payment practices
- ESRS E5 (Circular Economy) ← ISO 20400 lifecycle perspective + end-of-life considerations
