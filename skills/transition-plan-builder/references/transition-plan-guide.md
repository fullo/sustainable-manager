# Transition Plan Guide

Reference material for building credible climate transition plans, aligned with the Transition Plan Taskforce (TPT) Framework, ESRS E1-1 (revised ESRS 2026), and the SBTi Corporate Net-Zero Standard. Last verified: July 2026.

**Version note**: the SBTi released Corporate Net-Zero Standard **v2.0 in June 2026** (company categorization, separate Scope 1/2 targets, revised Scope 3 approach, mandatory transition planning and assurance, expanded removals provisions). Validation timeline: v1.3.1 through 2026 → dual track from Q1 2027 → v2.0 mandatory for new submissions from 1 February 2028. The sector figures below reflect v1-era pathways; check current SBTi resources when targeting a v2.0 validation.

---

## 1. Structure (TPT Framework + ESRS E1-1)

### 1.1 Ambition
- Net-zero target year (no later than 2050)
- Interim targets (2025, 2030, 2035, 2040)
- SBTi commitment/validation status
- Alignment with Paris Agreement 1.5C pathway
- Scope coverage: which scopes and categories are included

### 1.2 Current State
- Base year and base year emissions (Scope 1, 2, 3)
- Emission trends (last 3-5 years)
- Intensity metrics (tCO2e per revenue, per employee, per unit)
- Sector average comparison
- Current initiatives already in progress
- Data quality assessment (% measured vs estimated)

### 1.3 Decarbonization Strategy
- Levers by scope (detailed in Section 4 below)
- Technology roadmap: which technologies, when available, adoption timeline
- CapEx plan: investment by lever, by year
- OpEx implications: ongoing costs of new technologies/processes
- Revenue opportunities: green products, services, markets

### 1.4 Implementation
- Governance structure (board oversight, management ownership)
- Organizational changes required
- Milestones: specific, measurable, time-bound actions
- Resource allocation: budget, headcount, capabilities
- Risk management: technology risk, policy risk, market risk

### 1.5 Engagement
- Supplier engagement: Scope 3 reduction through supply chain collaboration
- Customer engagement: demand-side solutions, product portfolio shifts
- Policy advocacy: alignment with climate policy, industry associations
- Industry collaboration: pre-competitive partnerships, sector initiatives
- Financial engagement: green financing, sustainability-linked instruments

### 1.6 Metrics & Monitoring
- KPIs: absolute emissions, intensity, renewable energy %, CapEx alignment
- Reporting cadence: annual public reporting, quarterly internal review
- Assurance: third-party verification of emissions data and progress
- Target reassessment: biennial review against latest climate science
- Accountability: consequences for missing targets

## 2. SBTi Sectoral Pathways

Required emission reduction trajectories by sector. All figures are minimum reductions from base year to meet 1.5C alignment.

### Power & Energy
- **Required**: 80-100% reduction in absolute emissions by 2030 (1.5C)
- Coal phase-out: by 2030 in OECD, by 2040 globally
- Renewable share: >80% of generation by 2030
- Grid intensity: <50 gCO2/kWh by 2030
- Key levers: renewable capacity build-out, coal retirement, storage, grid modernization

### Transport — Road
- **Required**: ~35% reduction in well-to-wheel intensity by 2030
- Fleet electrification: 100% of new passenger vehicle sales electric by 2035
- Heavy-duty vehicles: electrification + hydrogen for long-haul
- Logistics: route optimization, modal shift (road to rail)
- Key levers: EV fleet transition, biofuels as bridge, efficiency improvements

### Buildings
- **Required**: ~45-50% reduction in energy intensity (kWh/m2) by 2030
- Deep renovation: 3% annual renovation rate of existing stock
- New buildings: near-zero energy (NZEB) standard
- Heat pump deployment: replace fossil fuel heating
- Key levers: insulation, heat pumps, smart building management, on-site renewables

### Chemicals
- **Required**: ~25-30% reduction in absolute emissions by 2030
- Process electrification: e-crackers, heat pumps for low-grade heat
- Feedstock: bio-based and recycled feedstock
- Energy: renewable electricity and green hydrogen for high-grade heat
- CCS: for process emissions where no alternative exists
- Key levers: electrification, feedstock switch, energy efficiency, CCS for process

### Cement
- **Required**: ~20-25% reduction in Scope 1 intensity (kgCO2/t clinker) by 2030
- Clinker ratio reduction: supplementary cementitious materials (SCMs)
- Alternative fuels: biomass, waste-derived fuels
- Energy efficiency: WHR (waste heat recovery), grinding optimization
- Long-term: carbon capture (post-2030 for significant contribution)
- Key levers: clinker substitution, alternative fuels, energy efficiency, CCS (post-2030)

### Steel
- **Required**: ~25-30% reduction in Scope 1+2 intensity by 2030
- BF-BOF to EAF transition: electric arc furnace with scrap
- DRI-H2: direct reduced iron with green hydrogen (scaling post-2025)
- Energy efficiency: existing BF-BOF optimization
- Key levers: scrap-based EAF, DRI-H2, energy efficiency, renewable electricity

### Food & Agriculture
- **Required**: ~30% reduction in absolute land use emissions by 2030
- Deforestation: zero deforestation in commodity supply chains by 2025
- Methane: enteric fermentation reduction, manure management
- Fertilizer: precision agriculture, organic fertilizer, nitrification inhibitors
- Yield improvement: sustainable intensification
- Key levers: deforestation-free sourcing, methane reduction, precision ag, diet shift

### Apparel & Textiles
- **Required**: ~45% reduction in absolute Scope 1+2 emissions by 2030
- Manufacturing: renewable energy in Tier 1-2 suppliers
- Material efficiency: recycled content, longer product life, reduced waste
- Transport: logistics optimization, nearshoring
- Consumer: circular business models, take-back schemes
- Key levers: renewable energy in supply chain, material innovation, circularity

## 3. Decarbonization Levers — Detailed

### Scope 1 Levers
| Lever | Reduction Potential | Timeline | Cost Range | Technology Readiness |
|-------|-------------------|----------|-----------|---------------------|
| Energy efficiency (process) | 10-30% of process emissions | 1-3 years | Low-Medium | Mature |
| Fuel switching (gas to biogas/hydrogen) | 30-80% of combustion emissions | 3-7 years | Medium-High | Emerging (H2) / Mature (biogas) |
| Process electrification | 50-100% of thermal process | 3-10 years | High | Varies by temperature |
| Heat pumps (low-grade heat) | 50-80% of heating emissions | 1-5 years | Medium | Mature |
| Fleet electrification | 60-100% of vehicle emissions | 3-7 years | Medium-High | Mature (LDV) / Emerging (HDV) |
| Carbon capture (CCUS) | 85-95% of point source | 5-10 years | Very High | Demonstration/Early commercial |
| Fugitive emission reduction | 30-80% of fugitives | 1-3 years | Low-Medium | Mature |

## 4. Credibility Criteria

### Must-have elements for a credible transition plan
1. **Science-based**: aligned with 1.5C pathway, ideally SBTi-validated
2. **Comprehensive**: covers Scope 1, 2, and material Scope 3 categories
3. **Near-term action**: specific milestones for 2025-2030 (not just 2050 aspirations)
4. **No near-term offset reliance**: offsets only for residual emissions post-2040/2050
5. **CapEx-aligned**: investment plan demonstrably supports the reduction trajectory
6. **Governance-integrated**: board oversight, management accountability, compensation linkage
7. **Transparent**: publicly disclosed, annually updated, third-party verified
8. **Just transition**: addresses workforce implications, community impacts
9. **Policy-coherent**: no lobbying against climate policy
10. **Adaptive**: built-in review mechanisms to update based on new science/technology

### Red flags (indicators of non-credible plans)
- Net-zero target without interim milestones
- Heavy reliance on unproven technology at scale (CCS for >30% of reductions before 2035)
- Offsets substituting for reduction in near/medium term
- Scope 3 excluded from targets despite being >40% of footprint
- No CapEx alignment (business-as-usual investment pattern)
- Lack of governance structure or accountability mechanisms
- Lobbying against climate policy while claiming climate commitment
- Intensity targets only (allows absolute growth)
- Green revenue targets without emission reduction targets

## 5. Common Pitfalls

### Strategic pitfalls
- Setting a long-term target (2050) without defining the near-term path (2025-2030)
- Assuming technology breakthroughs will solve the problem (e.g., affordable CCS, DAC at scale)
- Relying on market-based Scope 2 accounting (RECs) without actual energy system change
- Treating the transition plan as a sustainability exercise rather than a business strategy
- Not quantifying the investment required (CapEx/OpEx)

### Technical pitfalls
- Base year not representative (e.g., COVID year, atypical production)
- Inconsistent boundaries between GHG inventory and transition plan
- Double-counting reductions across levers
- Not accounting for growth (absolute emissions may increase even with intensity improvements)
- Ignoring rebound effects (efficiency gains consumed by increased activity)

### Communication pitfalls
- Claiming "carbon neutral" based on offsets without disclosing scope
- Cherry-picking intensity metrics that show improvement while absolute emissions grow
- Announcing net-zero without disclosing the role of offsets/removals
- Not disclosing assumptions and uncertainties

## 6. Template Structure

A complete transition plan document should contain:

1. **Executive Summary** (1-2 pages)
2. **Ambition & Targets** (2-3 pages): net-zero commitment, interim targets, SBTi status
3. **Current State** (3-5 pages): baseline emissions, trends, sector context
4. **Decarbonization Strategy** (5-10 pages): levers, technology roadmap, CapEx plan
5. **Implementation Plan** (3-5 pages): milestones, actions, responsibilities, timeline
6. **Governance** (2-3 pages): oversight, accountability, compensation linkage
7. **Engagement** (2-3 pages): suppliers, customers, policy, industry
8. **Risk & Opportunities** (2-3 pages): climate-related risks and business opportunities
9. **Metrics & Monitoring** (2-3 pages): KPIs, reporting, assurance
10. **Appendices**: detailed emission inventory, methodology, assumptions, glossary

## 7. Financial Planning for Transition

### CapEx Categories
| Category | Examples | Typical Payback | Emission Reduction |
|----------|---------|----------------|-------------------|
| Energy efficiency | LED, insulation, VSD, heat recovery | 2-5 years | 10-30% of energy-related |
| Electrification | Heat pumps, e-boilers, EV fleet | 5-10 years | 30-80% of fuel-related |
| Renewable energy | Solar PV, wind (on-site), storage | 5-12 years | 50-100% of Scope 2 |
| Process change | New equipment, material substitution | 7-15 years | Variable |
| Carbon capture | CCS/CCUS infrastructure | 10-20 years | 85-95% of point source |
| Supplier programs | Engagement, capacity building, co-investment | 3-7 years | 10-30% of Scope 3 |

### Financing Instruments
- **Green bonds**: for large-scale capital investments (>50M EUR)
- **Sustainability-linked loans (SLL)**: margin step-down tied to climate KPIs
- **EU Innovation Fund**: grants for innovative low-carbon technologies
- **LIFE Programme**: EU co-financing for environmental/climate projects
- **National incentives**: country-specific (see Italian context reference)
- **Internal carbon price**: earmark revenue for transition investments
- **ETS revenue recycling**: use allowance costs as signal for internal investment

### ROI Calculation Framework
For each decarbonization lever, calculate:
1. **Direct financial savings**: energy cost reduction, material efficiency
2. **Carbon price avoidance**: EU ETS allowance savings (current ~80 EUR/tCO2e, projected to rise)
3. **Incentive capture**: tax credits, subsidies, green certificates
4. **Risk reduction value**: stranded asset avoidance, regulatory compliance
5. **Revenue uplift**: green premium pricing, new market access
6. **Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)**: full lifecycle cost comparison vs. BAU

## 8. Just Transition Considerations

### Workforce Impact Assessment
- Identify roles and functions affected by the transition
- Map skills gaps: current workforce skills vs. future needs
- Develop reskilling/upskilling programs
- Engage with trade unions and worker representatives
- Ensure fair treatment of affected workers (redeployment, severance, support)

### Community Impact
- Assess impacts on communities dependent on carbon-intensive operations
- Develop community transition plans for facility closures or conversions
- Invest in local economic diversification
- Engage with local government and civil society

### Supply Chain Transition
- Support SME suppliers in their own decarbonization journey
- Provide training, tools, and financial support where possible
- Avoid abrupt termination of supplier relationships due to ESG gaps
- Phased transition with clear communication and support

## 9. Monitoring & Reporting Cadence

### Quarterly Internal Review
- Track KPIs against milestones: absolute emissions, intensity, renewable %, CapEx spend
- Traffic light dashboard: green (on track), amber (at risk), red (off track)
- Escalation triggers: >10% deviation from quarterly target
- Action items and course corrections

### Annual Public Reporting
- ESRS E1-1 transition plan disclosure
- Progress against near-term and long-term targets
- Updated waterfall chart showing actual vs. planned reductions
- CapEx actual vs. planned
- Updated risk assessment

### Biennial Target Review
- Reassess targets against latest climate science (IPCC, IEA)
- Adjust trajectory if SBTi updates sector pathways
- Incorporate new technology developments
- Update financial assumptions (carbon price, energy prices, technology costs)

### External Verification
- Annual third-party verification of GHG inventory (limited or reasonable assurance)
- SBTi progress tracking (annual CDP disclosure for committed companies)
- Alignment with TCFD / ISSB S2 recommendations
- Peer benchmarking and ranking participation (CDP Climate, TPI)
