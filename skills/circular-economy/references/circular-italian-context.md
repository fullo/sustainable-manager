# Contesto Italiano per l'Economia Circolare

## CONAI System (Consorzio Nazionale Imballaggi)

### Overview
CONAI is the Italian national consortium for the recovery and recycling of packaging
materials. It is a private, non-profit consortium established under Legislative Decree
22/1997 (Ronchi Decree) and now governed by D.Lgs. 152/2006 (Testo Unico Ambientale).

CONAI coordinates 6 material-specific consortia, each responsible for the collection,
recovery, and recycling of a specific packaging material stream.

### Material-Specific Consortia

| Consortium | Material | Recycling Rate | Tonnes Managed | Notes |
|------------|----------|---------------|----------------|-------|
| COREPLA | Plastics | 48.6% recycling, 90% recovery | 8.7M tonnes | Largest by volume; includes energy recovery |
| COMIECO | Paper/Cardboard | 87% | 3.6M tonnes | Highest recycling rate among consortia |
| COREVE | Glass | 77% | 2.4M tonnes | Strong infrastructure in North Italy |
| RICREA | Steel | 82% | 0.5M tonnes | Includes tinplate cans and closures |
| CIAL | Aluminium | 70% | 0.07M tonnes | Growing volumes from beverage cans |
| RILEGNO | Wood | 64% | 3.0M tonnes | Includes pallets and crates |

### Contributo Ambientale CONAI (CAC)
- Environmental contribution paid by producers and importers of packaging
- Differentiated by material and packaging type
- Fee modulation based on recyclability (introduced 2020):
  - Level A: recyclable with established infrastructure (lowest fee)
  - Level B: recyclable with emerging infrastructure
  - Level C: not currently recyclable (highest fee)
- CAC revenues fund collection and recycling infrastructure

## Codici CER (Catalogo Europeo Rifiuti)

### Structure
Italian implementation of the European Waste Catalogue (EWC) under Decision 2000/532/EC.
Six-digit codes structured as: Chapter (2 digits) - Subchapter (2 digits) - Code (2 digits).

### Key Categories for Circular Economy

| Chapter | Description | Common Codes |
|---------|-------------|--------------|
| 15 01 | Packaging waste | 15 01 01 (paper), 15 01 02 (plastic), 15 01 07 (glass) |
| 17 | Construction and demolition | 17 01 (concrete), 17 02 (wood/glass/plastic) |
| 19 | Waste treatment facilities | 19 12 (mechanical treatment), 19 05 (composting) |
| 20 | Municipal waste | 20 01 (separately collected), 20 03 (other municipal) |

### Special Waste vs Municipal Waste
- **Rifiuti urbani** (municipal): generated by households and similar
- **Rifiuti speciali** (special/industrial): generated by industrial/commercial activities
- Classification determines responsibility: municipalities vs producers
- Special waste requires tracciabilita (traceability) via RENTRI system (from 2025)

## National Targets and Strategy

### Italy Recycling Performance (2022 Data)
- Overall recycling rate: 72% (well above EU average of ~48%)
- Municipal waste recycling: 65%
- Packaging recycling: 73.3%
- Italy ranks 2nd in EU for circular economy index (after Netherlands)

### PNGR (Piano Nazionale Gestione Rifiuti)
- National waste management plan adopted 2022
- Key targets:
  - Reduce per-capita waste generation
  - Achieve 65% municipal waste recycling by 2035
  - Limit landfill to max 10% of municipal waste by 2035
  - Close the North-South infrastructure gap

### Strategia Nazionale per l'Economia Circolare (2022)
- National circular economy strategy adopted June 2022
- Priority sectors: construction, textiles, plastics, WEEE, food
- Key actions:
  - Tax incentives for circular business models
  - Green public procurement expansion
  - Innovation support for circular technologies
  - Traceability and digital product passport development

### MUD (Modello Unico di Dichiarazione Ambientale)
- Annual environmental declaration required for waste producers/managers
- Reports waste generation, treatment, and disposal data
- Submitted to local Chamber of Commerce by April 30 each year
- Data feeds into national waste statistics

## Italian Circular Economy Strengths

### Industrial Symbiosis Districts
- **Emilia-Romagna**: leading region for industrial symbiosis networks
  - ASTER network connecting research and industry
  - Waste exchange platforms between companies
- **Lombardy**: strong recycling infrastructure and circular innovation
  - Milan as circular economy hub
  - Montello recycling district (Bergamo)
- **Veneto**: advanced waste collection systems (pay-as-you-throw)
  - Treviso model: >85% separate collection rate
  - Contarina SpA as international best practice

### International Recognition
- CONAI model studied and replicated internationally
- Italy leads EU in material recycling (physical terms)
- Strong tradition in industrial districts facilitating material loops
- Fashion/textile sector circular innovation (Prato district for textile recycling)

## Incentives and Funding

### Credito d'Imposta per Economia Circolare
- Tax credit for investments in circular economy projects
- Covers: recycled material use, product redesign for circularity, reverse logistics
- Up to 36% of eligible investment costs

### Fondo Innovazione
- Fund supporting circular business model innovation
- Grants for startups and SMEs developing circular solutions
- Focus areas: sharing economy, product-as-a-service, remanufacturing

### PNRR Resources (Piano Nazionale Ripresa e Resilienza)
- Mission 2, Component 1: Green revolution and ecological transition
- EUR 2.1 billion for waste management and circular economy
- Investments in:
  - Mechanical-biological treatment plants
  - Separate collection infrastructure (especially in South)
  - Recycling hubs for specific material streams
  - Flagship circular economy projects

### Green Public Procurement (CAM)
- Criteri Ambientali Minimi: mandatory green criteria for public procurement
- Italy is the only EU country with mandatory GPP for all public contracts
- CAM cover: construction, textiles, furniture, IT, cleaning, food services
- Require minimum recycled content, durability, end-of-life recovery

## Common Challenges for Italian Companies

### Regional Disparities
- **North-South gap**: separate collection rate varies from >70% (North) to <40% (some Southern regions)
- Infrastructure availability differs significantly by region
- Cost of waste management varies up to 3x between regions
- Permitting complexity differs by region and municipality

### Material Stream Gaps
- Soft/flexible plastics: limited recycling infrastructure
- Composite materials: difficult to separate and recycle
- Multi-layer packaging: recyclability challenges
- Textiles: growing volumes but nascent recycling capacity
- Construction and demolition waste: quality of recycled aggregates

### SME-Specific Challenges
- **Scale**: small companies lack bargaining power for recycled material procurement
- **Data**: limited resources for material flow tracking and reporting
- **Expertise**: circular economy knowledge gap, especially for micro-enterprises
- **Investment**: high upfront costs for circular transitions with uncertain ROI
- **Supply chain**: difficulty tracing recycled content claims upstream

### Traceability and Certification
- Chain of custody for recycled content: mass balance vs physical segregation
- Certification schemes: Plastica Seconda Vita, Remade in Italy, EU Ecolabel
- RENTRI (Registro Elettronico Nazionale Tracciabilita Rifiuti): digital waste traceability
  - Replacing SISTRI system
  - Phased rollout starting 2025
  - Mandatory for waste producers, transporters, and treatment facilities
- Greenwashing risk: need for verified claims and third-party audits
