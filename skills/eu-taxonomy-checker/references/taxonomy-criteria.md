# EU Taxonomy Technical Reference

Comprehensive reference for the EU Taxonomy Regulation (EU) 2020/852, the Climate Delegated Act (EU) 2021/2139, the Environmental Delegated Act (EU) 2023/2486, and the Disclosures Delegated Act (EU) 2021/2178, as amended by the Simplification Delegated Regulation (EU) 2026/73 (10% materiality threshold, simplified templates, optional deferral for financial undertakings). Last verified: July 2026.

---

## The 6 Environmental Objectives

The EU Taxonomy defines six environmental objectives under Article 9 of Regulation (EU) 2020/852:

1. **Climate change mitigation** (Art. 10) — Activities that contribute substantially to stabilisation of GHG concentrations consistent with the Paris Agreement long-term temperature goal of 1.5C.
2. **Climate change adaptation** (Art. 11) — Activities that include adaptation solutions reducing material physical climate risks, or enabling activities that contribute to adaptation.
3. **Sustainable use and protection of water and marine resources** (Art. 12) — Activities contributing to achieving good status of water bodies or preventing deterioration, in line with the EU Water Framework Directive.
4. **Transition to a circular economy** (Art. 13) — Activities supporting resource efficiency, waste prevention, reuse, recycling, and extended product life.
5. **Pollution prevention and control** (Art. 14) — Activities contributing to preventing or reducing pollutant emissions to air, water, and land, minimising adverse impacts on human health and the environment.
6. **Protection and restoration of biodiversity and ecosystems** (Art. 15) — Activities contributing to protecting, conserving, or restoring biodiversity and ecosystem services.

### Interaction between objectives
An activity can make a Substantial Contribution to ONE objective while doing no significant harm (DNSH) to the OTHER FIVE. An activity may also be eligible under multiple objectives but must choose one for alignment purposes per reporting period.

## Key Sectors and Technical Screening Criteria (TSC)

### Energy (NACE D35)

#### Electricity generation from solar photovoltaic (Activity 4.1)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):** Lifecycle GHG emissions < 100 gCO2e/kWh
- **Notes:** Solar PV generally meets this threshold automatically; documentation of lifecycle assessment may be required.
- **Enabling activity:** YES — enables decarbonisation of other sectors
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Adaptation: Climate risk assessment per Appendix A
  - Water: N/A
  - Circular economy: Waste management plan, recyclability assessment for panels
  - Pollution: Compliance with hazardous substance restrictions (e.g., cadmium in thin-film)
  - Biodiversity: EIA where required; avoid significant impact on protected areas

#### Electricity generation from wind power (Activity 4.3)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):** Automatically meets TSC (lifecycle GHG inherently below threshold)
- **Enabling activity:** YES
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Adaptation: Climate risk assessment per Appendix A
  - Water: N/A
  - Circular economy: Waste management plan including blade recyclability
  - Pollution: Noise and electromagnetic interference within limits
  - Biodiversity: EIA mandatory; mitigate bird/bat collision risk; avoid Natura 2000 sites unless Appropriate Assessment permits

#### Electricity generation from natural gas (Activity 4.29 — transitional)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Direct emissions < 270 gCO2e/kWh of electricity output, OR
  - Annual average < 550 kgCO2e/kW of capacity over 20 years
  - Must replace a higher-emitting facility (coal/oil)
  - Construction permit obtained before 31 December 2030
  - Must be technically capable of switching to low-carbon gases (H2, biogas) by 2035
  - Must publish annual monitoring of transition plan
- **Transitional activity:** YES — subject to Article 10(2) conditions
- **DNSH requirements:** Full DNSH compliance required, including methane leakage monitoring

#### Cogeneration from natural gas (Activity 4.30 — transitional)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Same emission thresholds as 4.29
  - High-efficiency cogeneration as per Directive 2012/27/EU
  - Combined heat and power efficiency > relevant thresholds
- **Transitional activity:** YES

#### Electricity generation from hydropower (Activity 4.5)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Run-of-river without reservoir: lifecycle GHG < 100 gCO2e/kWh
  - With reservoir: power density > 5 W/m2 OR lifecycle GHG < 100 gCO2e/kWh
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Water: Full compliance with Water Framework Directive, including ecological flow
  - Biodiversity: EIA mandatory; fish passage requirements

#### Electricity generation from bioenergy (Activity 4.8)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - GHG savings >= 80% compared to fossil comparator (per RED II methodology)
  - Feedstock sustainability criteria per Directive (EU) 2018/2001
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Biodiversity: Must not use feedstock from high-biodiversity land
  - Pollution: BAT-AEL for emissions to air

### Manufacturing (NACE C)

#### Manufacture of cement (Activity 3.7)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Grey cement clinker: < 0.498 tCO2e per tonne of clinker
  - Alternative: Cement/binder with lifecycle GHG below threshold relative to regional benchmark
- **Notes:** Includes Scope 1 (process + fuel) emissions; Scope 2 if electricity is used in kilns
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: BAT-AEL for dust, NOx, SOx, HCl, HF, heavy metals
  - Circular economy: Alternative fuels and raw materials usage tracked
  - Water: Water management plan if in water-stressed area

#### Manufacture of iron and steel (Activity 3.9)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Electric Arc Furnace (EAF): < 0.283 tCO2e per tonne of crude steel
  - Blast Furnace-Basic Oxygen Furnace (BF-BOF): emissions must meet benchmark, with conditions on decarbonisation pathway
  - Includes direct reduced iron (DRI) routes
- **Notes:** Covers Scope 1 and relevant Scope 2 emissions from steelmaking process
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: BAT-AEL per BREF for Iron and Steel
  - Water: Water recycling systems, no discharge of untreated wastewater
  - Circular economy: Slag and by-product recovery plan

#### Manufacture of aluminium (Activity 3.8)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Primary aluminium: < 1.484 tCO2e per tonne of aluminium
  - Includes Scope 1 + Scope 2 (indirect from electricity, which is the dominant source)
  - Secondary (recycled) aluminium has significantly lower thresholds
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: PFC emissions minimised; BAT-AEL compliance
  - Water: Red mud management for bauxite refining

#### Manufacture of ceramics (Activity 3.12 — not explicitly listed; mapped via 3.7 analogues)
- **Note:** Ceramics are partially covered. Key products include:
  - Bricks: specific emission thresholds per tonne of product
  - Tiles and sanitary ware: thresholds vary by product type
  - Refractory products: assessed against BAT-AEL
- **Italian context:** Major relevance for the Sassuolo ceramic district
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: Dust, fluoride, and lead emissions within BAT-AEL
  - Water: Process water recycling

#### Manufacture of plastics in primary form (Activity 3.17)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Must be produced wholly or partially from renewable feedstock
  - Lifecycle GHG below fossil-based comparator
- **Circular economy objective alternative:**
  - Chemical or mechanical recycling meeting quality and traceability requirements

### Buildings (NACE F41, F43)

#### Construction of new buildings (Activity 7.1)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Primary Energy Demand (PED) at least 10% below the nearly zero-energy building (NZEB) threshold set by national regulation
  - For buildings > 5,000 m2: air-tightness and thermal integrity testing post-construction
  - EPC class A, OR in top 15% of national building stock by primary energy demand
  - GWP calculated for lifecycle stages A1-A3 (for buildings > 5,000 m2)
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Adaptation: Climate risk assessment; resilience measures for identified risks
  - Water: Water flow rates for taps <= 6 L/min, showers <= 8 L/min, WCs <= 6 L/flush
  - Circular economy: >= 70% (by weight) of non-hazardous CDW prepared for reuse/recycling
  - Pollution: No substances of very high concern (SVHC); formaldehyde < 0.06 mg/m3; VOC within limits
  - Biodiversity: Not built on arable/greenfield land of recognised high biodiversity value; EIA where required

#### Renovation of existing buildings (Activity 7.2)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - At least 30% reduction in primary energy demand compared to pre-renovation baseline
  - Alternative: Compliance with cost-optimal minimum energy performance requirements for major renovation under EPBD
- **DNSH requirements:** Same as 7.1, adapted for renovation context

#### Installation, maintenance and repair of energy efficiency equipment (Activity 7.3)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Individual measures that comply with minimum requirements set in applicable building codes and EPBD implementing measures
  - Covers: insulation, windows/doors, heat pumps, lighting, building automation and control systems
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: Refrigerant GWP for heat pumps; insulation material restrictions

#### Installation of renewable energy technologies (Activity 7.6)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - On-site installation of solar PV, solar thermal, heat pumps, wind, geothermal
- **DNSH requirements:** Per technology-specific criteria

### Transport (NACE H49-51)

#### Passenger cars and light commercial vehicles (Activity 6.5)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Zero direct (tailpipe) CO2 emissions — effectively BEV or FCEV only
  - Category M1 and N1 vehicles
  - Until 2025: vehicles with < 50 gCO2/km were eligible as transitional; from 2026 this expires
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Circular economy: End-of-life vehicle directive compliance; battery recycling plan
  - Pollution: Tyre and brake particulate matter; RDE compliance

#### Freight transport by road (Activity 6.6)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Zero direct CO2 emissions for heavy-duty vehicles (> 3.5t)
  - From 2026: 0 gCO2/km for vehicles > 7.5t
  - Transitional: Vehicles meeting Euro VI and specific CO2/tkm thresholds
- **DNSH requirements:** Similar to 6.5

#### Passenger and freight rail transport (Activity 6.1 / 6.2)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Zero direct tailpipe emissions (electric trains), OR
  - < 50 gCO2 per passenger-km for bimodal rolling stock on tracks where electrification is not feasible
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Pollution: Noise within TSI Noise limits
  - Biodiversity: EIA for new rail infrastructure

#### Inland waterways and maritime transport (Activity 6.7 / 6.10)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Zero direct emissions vessels, OR
  - Vessels with emissions below defined thresholds (hybrid/transitional criteria apply)
  - Maritime: Energy Efficiency Design Index (EEDI) 10% below requirement

### ICT (NACE J61-63)

#### Data processing, hosting, and related activities — Data centres (Activity 8.1)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE) < 1.5 (expected to tighten over time)
  - European Code of Conduct for Data Centres implemented
  - GHG emissions from refrigerants: GWP < 675 for cooling
  - Renewable energy sourcing or PPA in place
  - Waste heat reuse where feasible
- **Enabling activity:** YES
- **DNSH requirements:**
  - Water: Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE) thresholds for water-cooled systems
  - Circular economy: WEEE compliance; server/hardware reuse programme

#### Data-driven solutions for GHG emission reductions (Activity 8.2)
- **Substantial Contribution (Climate Mitigation):**
  - ICT solution must demonstrably enable GHG reductions in other economic activities
  - Lifecycle assessment showing net positive climate impact
- **Enabling activity:** YES
- **Notes:** Broad category covering smart grid, building management, precision agriculture, logistics optimisation

## DNSH Criteria Summary Matrix

For each activity making a Substantial Contribution to a given objective, the DNSH criteria for the remaining five objectives must be satisfied.

| Activity Sector | SC Objective | Adaptation DNSH | Water DNSH | Circular Economy DNSH | Pollution DNSH | Biodiversity DNSH |
|----------------|-------------|-----------------|------------|----------------------|----------------|-------------------|
| Energy (solar, wind) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment (Appendix A) | Generally N/A | Waste management plan, panel recyclability | Hazardous substance restrictions | EIA where required |
| Energy (gas — transitional) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment | Water discharge limits | N/A | Methane leakage monitoring, BAT-AEL | EIA where required |
| Manufacturing (cement) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment | Water management plan | Alternative raw materials usage | BAT-AEL for dust, NOx, SOx | EIA where required |
| Manufacturing (steel) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment | Water recycling, no untreated discharge | Slag/by-product recovery | BAT-AEL per BREF | EIA where required |
| Buildings (new) | Mitigation | Climate resilience measures | Water flow rate limits | CDW reuse/recycling >= 70% | No SVHC, VOC limits | Not on high-biodiversity land |
| Buildings (renovation) | Mitigation | Climate resilience measures | Water flow rate limits | CDW reuse/recycling >= 70% | No SVHC, VOC limits | N/A (existing site) |
| Transport (BEV/FCEV) | Mitigation | N/A | N/A | ELV compliance, battery recycling | Tyre/brake PM, RDE | N/A |
| Transport (rail) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment | N/A | Material recycling | TSI Noise limits | EIA for new infrastructure |
| ICT (data centres) | Mitigation | Climate risk assessment | WUE thresholds | WEEE compliance, hardware reuse | Refrigerant GWP < 675 | N/A |

### Appendix A — Climate Risk and Vulnerability Assessment (for Adaptation DNSH)
Required for most activities. Must include:
1. Screening of climate-related hazards from the Appendix A list (acute and chronic)
2. Where material risks identified: vulnerability and risk assessment
3. Assessment of adaptation solutions to reduce identified risks
4. For physical assets with lifetime > 10 years: ensure resilience to projected climate scenarios (RCP 4.5 / RCP 8.5)
5. Solutions must not adversely affect adaptation efforts of others

## Minimum Safeguards

Minimum safeguards (Article 18 of Regulation 2020/852) are entity-level requirements that must be met for any Taxonomy alignment claim. They are based on four international frameworks:

### 1. OECD Guidelines for Multinational Enterprises (2011, updated 2023)
- Risk-based due diligence across operations and supply chains
- Covers: human rights, employment, environment, anti-corruption, consumer interests, disclosure, science and technology, competition, taxation
- 2023 update adds: climate change, biodiversity, technology/digitalisation, supply chain due diligence

### 2. UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights (UNGPs)
- Pillar I: State duty to protect human rights
- Pillar II: Corporate responsibility to respect human rights
- Pillar III: Access to remedy
- Requires: human rights policy, due diligence process, remediation mechanisms

### 3. ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work
- Freedom of association and collective bargaining (Conventions 87, 98)
- Elimination of forced or compulsory labour (Conventions 29, 105)
- Abolition of child labour (Conventions 138, 182)
- Elimination of discrimination in employment (Conventions 100, 111)
- Safe and healthy working environment (Conventions 155, 187)

### 4. International Bill of Human Rights
- Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)
- International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights (ICCPR)
- International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights (ICESCR)

### Practical Verification Checklist
The Platform on Sustainable Finance (Final Report on Minimum Safeguards, October 2022) recommends checking:

| Area | Check | Evidence Required |
|------|-------|-------------------|
| Human rights | Due diligence process in place? | Policy document, process description |
| Human rights | Covers own operations + supply chain? | Scope documentation |
| Labour rights | Freedom of association respected? | Policy, no union-busting history |
| Labour rights | No forced or child labour? | Supply chain audits, certifications |
| Anti-corruption | Anti-bribery/corruption policy? | Policy, training records |
| Anti-corruption | No convictions in last 5 years? | Legal records check |
| Taxation | No aggressive tax planning? | Tax transparency report, no tax haven controversies |
| Grievance mechanism | Accessible complaint mechanism? | Description, accessibility evidence |
| Controversies | No severe open controversies? | ESG controversy screening (UNGP severity criteria) |

### Severity Assessment for Controversies
A "severe" controversy (which would fail minimum safeguards) involves:
- Scale: large number of individuals affected
- Scope: irremediable or hard-to-remedy impacts
- Irremediability: cannot be fully restored
- Company response: inadequate response or denial

## Eligibility vs. Alignment: Understanding the Gap

### Current Market Data (approximate ranges)
- **Eligibility rates:** 36-46% of revenues/CapEx for large EU corporates
- **Alignment rates:** 10-16% of revenues/CapEx for the same corporates
- **Gap:** ~25-30 percentage points between eligibility and alignment

### Common Blockers Preventing Alignment

| Blocker | Frequency | Typical Sectors |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| Missing DNSH evidence (especially adaptation climate risk assessment) | Very High | All sectors |
| No minimum safeguard documentation | High | Manufacturing, transport |
| TSC thresholds not met (transitional criteria) | High | Energy (gas), manufacturing |
| Insufficient data granularity (asset-level vs. entity-level) | High | Multi-activity companies |
| Mixed-use assets (partially eligible) | Medium | Real estate, infrastructure |
| Supply chain data gaps for DNSH | Medium | Manufacturing, construction |
| Interpretation uncertainty (e.g., "top 15%" for buildings) | Medium | Real estate |
| Transitional TSC expiry (e.g., gas phase-out dates) | Growing | Energy |

### Pathway from Eligibility to Alignment
1. **Quick wins:** Document existing DNSH compliance; many companies already comply but lack formal evidence
2. **Medium effort:** Conduct climate risk assessments (Appendix A); implement grievance mechanisms; formalise human rights due diligence
3. **High effort:** Invest in technology upgrades to meet TSC thresholds; restructure supply chain due diligence
4. **Strategic:** Shift CapEx towards aligned activities; phase out non-alignable transitional activities before deadline

## Disclosure Requirements (Art. 8 — Disclosures Delegated Act)

### Non-financial undertakings (NFRD/CSRD scope)
Must disclose:
- **Turnover KPI:** Share of Taxonomy-aligned turnover / total turnover
- **CapEx KPI:** Share of Taxonomy-aligned CapEx / total CapEx
- **OpEx KPI:** Share of Taxonomy-aligned OpEx / total OpEx (denominator = non-capitalised direct costs related to R&D, building renovation, short-term leases, maintenance)

### Financial undertakings
- **Credit institutions:** Green Asset Ratio (GAR) — share of Taxonomy-aligned exposures / total covered assets
- **Insurance companies:** Taxonomy-aligned investment ratio and underwriting ratio
- **Asset managers:** Share of Taxonomy-aligned investments in AuM

### Reporting templates
Mandatory Annex templates from Delegated Regulation (EU) 2021/2178 (as amended):
- Template for Turnover, CapEx, OpEx by activity and objective
- Contextual information on methodology, assumptions, data sources
- Compliance with double-counting avoidance rules (activity can be reported under only one objective)
