# EU Taxonomy — Italian Context

Reference for Italy-specific regulatory requirements, sector characteristics, and implementation guidance for the EU Taxonomy.

---

## Regulatory Framework in Italy

### Consob Communication on Art. 8 Taxonomy Disclosure
- **Consob Communication No. 1/2022** provides guidance on Taxonomy-related disclosures in non-financial statements (Dichiarazione Non Finanziaria, DNF) under D.Lgs. 254/2016.
- Key requirements:
  - Entities subject to NFRD (now transitioning to CSRD) must include Taxonomy KPIs (Turnover, CapEx, OpEx) in their DNF.
  - Consob expects a clear reconciliation between Taxonomy-eligible and Taxonomy-aligned figures.
  - Narrative explanation of methodology, assumptions, and data sources is mandatory.
  - Consob has signalled increased scrutiny on the quality and granularity of Taxonomy disclosures starting from FY2023 reports.
- **Consob attention points:**
  - Consistency between Taxonomy disclosures and the rest of the DNF/sustainability report.
  - Adequate explanation of why eligible activities are not aligned (gap narrative).
  - Proper application of the CapEx plan provisions (Art. 8 allows reporting CapEx for activities not yet aligned if a credible transition plan exists).

### Banca d'Italia Expectations for Financial Institutions
- **Aspettative di vigilanza su rischi climatici e ambientali** (April 2022): Banca d'Italia expects banks and financial institutions to integrate ESG risks into governance, risk management, and disclosure.
- **ECB/SSM Pillar 2 context:** Italian significant institutions supervised by ECB must report Taxonomy alignment as part of ESG risk disclosures under Pillar 3.
- **Green Asset Ratio (GAR):** Italian banks are progressively disclosing GAR. Key challenges:
  - SME exposure: Large share of Italian bank portfolios consists of SME lending, where counterparties do not report Taxonomy data.
  - Retail mortgages: EPC data availability for residential properties is uneven across Italian regions.
  - Sovereign exposure: Excluded from GAR numerator, creating optical distortion for banks with large BTP portfolios.
- **Banca d'Italia thematic review findings:**
  - Data quality remains the primary challenge.
  - Use of estimates and proxies is accepted but must be transparent and conservative.
  - Forward-looking alignment plans are expected to support CapEx KPI credibility.

### IVASS (Insurance Supervisory Authority)
- Italian insurers must disclose Taxonomy-aligned investment ratios.
- IVASS aligns with EIOPA guidance on Taxonomy integration in ORSA and investment policies.
- Particular focus on real estate and infrastructure investment alignment.

## Italian Sectors with High Taxonomy Relevance

### Ceramics — Distretto di Sassuolo (Emilia-Romagna)
- **NACE codes:** C23.3 (manufacture of clay building materials), C23.4 (manufacture of other porcelain and ceramic products)
- **Eligibility:** Partially eligible under manufacturing activities (Activity 3.7 analogues and dedicated ceramic entries)
- **Key TSC challenges:**
  - Emission intensity thresholds for tile production: specific CO2/tonne thresholds apply
  - Energy source: high dependence on natural gas for kilns; electrification is technically complex
  - BAT-AEL compliance for fluoride, lead, and dust emissions
- **DNSH considerations:**
  - Water recycling rates in the district are already high (>90% in many plants)
  - Circular economy: high use of recycled raw materials in some product lines
- **Strategic note:** The Sassuolo district is exploring hydrogen-ready kilns and carbon capture as pathways to alignment. Confindustria Ceramica actively engages with EU institutions on feasibility of TSC thresholds.

### Automotive — Piedmont (Piemonte)
- **NACE codes:** C29.1 (manufacture of motor vehicles), C29.3 (manufacture of parts and accessories)
- **Eligibility:** Vehicle manufacturing is eligible; parts manufacturing has partial eligibility depending on end-use.
- **Key TSC challenges:**
  - Final assembly of zero-emission vehicles (BEV/FCEV) meets TSC; ICE vehicle manufacturing does not.
  - Component manufacturers: eligible only if components are exclusively for zero-emission vehicles or contribute to enabling activities.
- **Regional context:** Stellantis transition in Turin/Melfi; growing EV supply chain in Piedmont; Italvolt gigafactory project (subject to status updates).
- **Supply chain implications:** Tier 2/3 suppliers increasingly asked for Taxonomy data by OEMs.

### Energy — ENEL, ENI, and the Italian Energy Transition
- **ENEL:** High eligibility due to large renewable energy portfolio (solar, wind, hydro). Alignment rates among the highest in the European utility sector. CapEx heavily skewed towards aligned activities (grid modernisation, renewable capacity expansion).
- **ENI:** Complex Taxonomy profile due to oil & gas legacy. Eligible activities include:
  - Gas-fired power generation (transitional Activity 4.29/4.30 — subject to 2030 construction deadline)
  - Biofuels/biorefinery (eligible under bioenergy activities)
  - CCS/CCU projects (eligible under specific conditions)
  - Renewable energy projects (Plenitude subsidiary)
  - Hydrogen projects (eligible as enabling activities)
- **National context:** Italy's PNIEC (Piano Nazionale Integrato per l'Energia e il Clima) targets and PNRR (Piano Nazionale di Ripresa e Resilienza) investments are creating Taxonomy-aligned CapEx opportunities in renewables, grid, and energy efficiency.

### Fashion and Luxury — Limited Eligibility
- **NACE codes:** C13 (textiles), C14 (wearing apparel), C15 (leather)
- **Eligibility:** Very limited under current Taxonomy. Textile manufacturing is not explicitly listed in the Climate Delegated Act.
- **Partial eligibility paths:**
  - Circular economy objective (Environmental Delegated Act): textile reuse, repair, and recycling activities
  - Pollution prevention: activities reducing hazardous chemical use in production
  - Energy efficiency investments in manufacturing facilities (CapEx plan approach)
- **Italian context:** Major Italian fashion groups (Kering subsidiaries, Prada, Moncler, Brunello Cucinelli) report low Taxonomy eligibility but are investing in supply chain decarbonisation and circularity. Sustainability reporting under CSRD will increase pressure to identify eligible activities.

### Food and Agriculture — Limited but Growing
- **NACE codes:** A01-03 (agriculture, forestry, fishing), C10-12 (food manufacturing)
- **Eligibility:** Agriculture has some eligibility (forestry, land restoration). Food manufacturing has very limited direct eligibility.
- **Partial eligibility paths:**
  - Packaging: manufacturing of packaging from recycled/renewable materials (circular economy objective)
  - Transport and logistics: cold chain, fleet electrification
  - Energy efficiency: facility upgrades, renewable energy installations
  - Forestry: afforestation, forest management, conservation forestry
- **Italian context:** Barilla, Ferrero, Lavazza have limited direct Taxonomy eligibility but significant indirect exposure through supply chain and logistics.

## OIC Guidance on Taxonomy Disclosure

The Organismo Italiano di Contabilità (OIC) provides guidance relevant to Taxonomy reporting in Italian financial statements:
- **Integration with bilancio d'esercizio:** Taxonomy KPIs must be consistent with financial statement figures (turnover reconciled to conto economico, CapEx to investimenti in stato patrimoniale).
- **CapEx definition:** Aligned with IAS 16 (PP&E), IAS 38 (intangibles), IFRS 16 (right-of-use assets). For Italian GAAP reporters (OIC principles), analogous treatment applies.
- **OpEx definition:** Direct non-capitalised costs related to research and development, building renovation measures, short-term leases, maintenance, and repair. This is a narrower definition than general "operating expenses" in conto economico.
- **Rounding and materiality:** OIC guidance suggests disclosing Taxonomy KPIs as percentages rounded to one decimal place. Immaterial activities should still be disclosed if eligible.

## Confindustria Position Papers

Confindustria (the main Italian employers' federation) has published position papers on Taxonomy implementation:
- **Key positions:**
  - Advocating for recognition of transitional activities and transition pathways, especially for hard-to-abate sectors (ceramics, glass, steel).
  - Requesting proportionate requirements for SMEs in supply chains of large reporting companies.
  - Calling for clearer guidance on the Environmental Delegated Act criteria and sector-specific interpretations.
  - Emphasising the need for capacity building and technical support for Italian SMEs.
- **Sector associations engaged:**
  - Confindustria Ceramica (ceramics TSC feasibility)
  - Federacciai (steel decarbonisation pathways)
  - ANIE (electrical and electronic manufacturing)
  - Assoimmobiliare (real estate alignment challenges with Italian EPC framework)

## Common Pitfalls for Italian Companies

### SMEs in Supply Chains
- **The problem:** Large companies subject to CSRD/Taxonomy reporting are requesting Taxonomy data from their SME suppliers. Italian SMEs (often not directly subject to reporting obligations) lack resources and expertise to respond.
- **Practical issues:**
  - SMEs confuse eligibility with alignment, over-reporting alignment rates.
  - Missing DNSH documentation (especially climate risk assessments under Appendix A).
  - No formalised minimum safeguards (human rights due diligence, grievance mechanisms).
  - Difficulty calculating activity-level CapEx/OpEx/Revenue splits when operations are integrated.
- **Recommendations for Italian SMEs:**
  - Start with eligibility mapping: identify which NACE codes apply and whether activities are listed.
  - Conduct a simplified climate risk screening (physical risks relevant to Italian geography: flooding in Po valley, drought in the South, heatwaves).
  - Document existing environmental management (ISO 14001) and social practices as evidence for DNSH and minimum safeguards.
  - Engage with industry associations (Confindustria territoriale, CNA, Confapi) for shared tools and templates.
  - Use the CapEx plan approach: even if current operations are not aligned, demonstrate a credible investment plan towards alignment.

### EPC Data Challenges for Real Estate
- Italian EPC (APE — Attestato di Prestazione Energetica) data is managed at regional level with inconsistent databases.
- "Top 15% of national building stock" benchmark is difficult to establish due to fragmented data.
- ENEA provides some national statistics but granularity varies.
- Recommendation: use NZEB -10% threshold as the primary TSC path for new buildings; for existing buildings, rely on specific EPC class A documentation.

### Transitional Activities and Timeline Risk
- Italian companies heavily invested in gas infrastructure (co-generation, district heating) face timeline risk: transitional TSC requires construction permits before 31 December 2030 and transition to low-carbon fuels by 2035.
- Companies should document hydrogen/biomethane readiness plans and monitor evolving TSC thresholds.
