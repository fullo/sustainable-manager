Analyzing a manufacturer's report

A physical-goods producer lives in a different reality from a digital company — energy-intensive process emissions, raw materials that dominate Scope 3, an EPD on the product, packaging under the PPWR. Here we run the six-step report analysis on one.

The company: Ceramica Valdenza S.p.A.

AttributeValue
Sector / productPorcelain stoneware floor tiles (Sassuolo ceramic district)
Employees / revenue620 / €180M → below the post-Omnibus CSRD threshold (needs 1,000+ AND €450M) — the report is voluntary
ProcessGas-fired kilns (~1,200°C) → natural gas dominates Scope 1; large ceramic plants also sit under EU ETS
Product footprintPublishes an EPD (EN 15804) — functional unit 1 m² of tile
The report3rd voluntary "Bilancio di sostenibilità", declared "ispirato agli ESRS", GRI-referenced content index
MarketingA brochure line: "the first carbon-neutral tile collection"

Illustrative company; the numbers below show the shape of a real analysis, not a specific firm.

Step 1 — Framework reality check

The skill reads the methodological note and the GRI content index and reports the honest status:

Declared "inspired by ESRS" but reports with reference to GRI 2021, not in accordance. No double materiality assessment (ESRS 1). This is a voluntary report; VSME would be the proportionate standard for a company this size.
Teaching point — what does NOT apply. Ceramics is energy-intensive but is not a CBAM good (CBAM covers cement, steel, aluminium, fertilizers, hydrogen, electricity). The skill states this so you don't waste a quarter preparing for the wrong regulation — the mirror image of the steel-import scenario in the manual.

Every figure carries its source, so you (and your auditor) can jump straight to the page:

The Scope 2 gotcha, live: the report headlines "1,300 tCO2e" — the market-based figure. The location-based figure is 7× higher. The skill surfaces both and flags any headline that quotes only the flattering one.

PresentMissing (material for a tile maker)

Step 4 — Greenwashing ratings

ClaimRatingWhy
"The first carbon-neutral tile collection"MisleadingNeutrality rests on offsets, not on absolute reductions; no boundary or methodology for the collection. From 27 Sep 2026 the Empowering Consumers Directive bans offset-based neutrality claims outright.
"−40% CO2 since 2018"Partially substantiatedTrue for Scope 2 market-based; Scope 1 (the real mass) is roughly flat. Relative-vs-absolute.
"100% of process water recycled"UnsubstantiatedNo withdrawal or discharge figure to anchor the percentage.
EPD-based "−X kgCO2e per m² vs 2018"SubstantiatedThird-party-verified EPD, clear functional unit (1 m²), stated methodology.

The EPD line shows the flip side: when a claim rests on a verified LCA with a declared functional unit, the skill says so. Greenwashing detection is not blanket cynicism — it rewards substantiation.

Step 5 — Adversarial verification

Ask "verify your own analysis against the PDF" (or run /adversarial-verify). An independent pass re-opens each cited page and confirms value, unit, and location. In practice this catches page drift (right number, wrong page) and market/location mix-ups before they reach a board pack — exactly the slips that a single reading misses.

Step 6 — What to do next

  1. "Map our Scope 3 categories" → scope3-mapper puts first numbers on the missing cat. 1 raw materials (spend-based to start).
  2. "How circular is our packaging and product?" → circular-economy computes the MCI and checks PPWR for the pallet/shrink-wrap.
  3. "Rewrite the carbon-neutral claim so it survives the EmpCo Directive" → core skill proposes substantiable wording built on the EPD, not offsets.
  4. "Draft a transition plan for the kilns" → transition-plan-builder (electrification / hydrogen / heat recovery as levers).
The pattern, physical-goods edition: for a manufacturer the carbon truth lives in process energy (Scope 1) and raw materials (Scope 3 cat. 1) — not in the market-based Scope 2 number the brochure prefers. The skill keeps pulling the analysis back to where the mass actually is.
