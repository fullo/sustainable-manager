User Manual

A practical guide for first-time users. You don't need to master Claude Code — you only need to know what to ask and which skill answers.

Getting started

Skills activate in two ways:

  1. Automatically — write your question in plain language (English or Italian) and Claude activates the right skill from context. Example: "we import steel from Turkey, what do we need to do about CBAM?" activates cbam-compliance.
  2. Explicitly — invoke the skill with its slash command, e.g. /sustainable-manager:eu-regulation-matrix.

Every skill:

  • answers in your language — write in Italian and you get the Italian regulatory layer (Consob, ADM, MASE, CONAI, CAM, RAEE);
  • asks one question at a time — no need to prepare all your data upfront, the skill guides you. "I don't know" is a valid answer;
  • produces outputs that are drafts to review, not certifications: the final judgment belongs to you, your legal counsel, and your auditor.

Realistic starting scenarios

"We're an SME and our bank asked for ESG data"

You're not in CSRD scope (post-Omnibus it takes 1,000+ employees AND €450M turnover), but bank and customer requests are real. Start with eu-regulation-matrix to confirm what does NOT apply to you, then use the core skill citing the VSME standard: data requests from CSRD-scope customers cannot exceed that perimeter.

"Our German customer sent an 80-question questionnaire"

supplier-engagement can read and prioritize questionnaires (and tells you what you can legitimately decline if you have fewer than 1,000 employees). If it touches emissions, scope3-mapper produces first numbers with the spend-based method.

"We import 120 tonnes of steel profiles per year from Turkey"

cbam-compliance: you're above the de minimis threshold (50t cumulative), so you need authorized declarant status; the skill calculates embedded emissions and estimates certificates (purchases open February 2027, declaration due 30 September).

"The CFO wants to know what net-zero costs"

transition-plan-builder, six phases from baseline to CapEx plan. Mind the SBTi version: validations from 1 February 2028 must use the Corporate Net-Zero Standard v2.0.

"We have a data centre and 1,500 laptops; IT asks what to do"

sustainable-it-compliance — see the full case study below.

"Marketing wants to put 'carbon neutral platform' on the website"

Stop them, then use sustainable-it-compliance (IT claims) or the core skill (greenwashing detection): from 27 September 2026 the Empowering Consumers Directive bans offset-based neutrality claims.

How to start — just write:

We're an e-commerce company with 1,200 employees, a data centre in Milan and workloads on AWS. What sustainability obligations does our IT have?

Step 0 — Maturity snapshot

The skill does NOT start from obligations: it positions you first, with 4-8 questions on the SOFT pillars (Strategy, Implementation, Operations, Compliance), such as:

"Is sustainable IT in the IT strategy or ESG plan, with an owner and a budget?"

For Bottega Digitale the honest answer is: we measure little, no owner → Developing level. Practical effect: the skill doesn't bury you under 11 obligations — it starts from the 2-3 that bite now and from one measurement to switch on first (usually the cloud provider's carbon dashboard).

Step 3 — Measurement standards + tools

sci_calculator.py — measuring software

Bottega Digitale's checkout service consumes 120 kWh/month, runs on hardware with an embodied share of 45,000 gCO2e/month, and processes 1M requests. Command:

python3 skills/sustainable-it-compliance/scripts/sci_calculator.py \
  --energy-kwh 120 --region italy --embodied 45000 \
  --functional-units 1000000 --unit-name "API request"

Real output:

Boundary: cli boundary
  E (energy):            120.00 kWh
  I (grid intensity):    260 gCO2e/kWh
  E x I (operational):   31,200 gCO2e
  M (embodied):          45,000 gCO2e (provided)
  R (functional units):  1,000,000 (API request)
  SCI = ((E x I) + M)/R: 0.0762 gCO2e per API request
  Operational share:     40.9%

Three things to understand:

  1. 0.0762 gCO2e/request is a rate, not a total: it tells you whether next quarter's refactoring improves things. It must NOT be summed into the corporate inventory (that's what the GHG Protocol is for).
  2. The 40.9% operational share says embodied carbon outweighs energy here: optimizing code helps, extending hardware life helps more.
  3. If you don't know the embodied share, pass the four ISO formula parameters (embodied_detail: TE, TiR, EL, RS) and the script computes it — see --help.

EED checklist — the data centre report

Open references/eed-reporting-checklist.md and have facility + IT ops fill it in three passes: already measured / derivable / gap. For Bottega Digitale the typical picture emerges:

  • Total energy: 5.2 GWh ✓ (meter) — IT energy: 3.7 GWh ✓ (UPS output) → PUE 1.41
  • Water: not metered → gap; temporary workaround: allocated utility invoices, flagged as estimate
  • Reused heat: none → ERF = 0 — declare it, don't omit it
  • Renewables: 35% with Guarantees of Origin → REF 0.35

The report is due 15 May: the checklist exists precisely to surface instrumentation gaps months in advance, not the week before.

Cloud questionnaire — what AWS owes you

For the cloud side, references/cloud-provider-questionnaire.md (15 questions, EN+IT). The three that matter most here:

  • PUE/WUE per region (not a global average) → needed to choose between eu-south-1 and eu-west-1
  • Does the carbon dashboard cover embodied hardware or only allocated Scope 2?
  • Are renewable claims market-based with location-based disclosure too?

If the provider answers with global averages and brochure claims: that's a red flag to put in writing in the supplier assessment (the supplier-engagement skill uses this as Module F).

Step 4 — Gap analysis and device policy

For the device fleet the key number comes from the benchmark (assets/benchmarks/device-embodied-carbon.json): a business laptop ≈ 250 kgCO2e embodied. With 1,500 laptops:

  • 3-year refresh → 250/3 = 83 kgCO2e/year per laptop
  • 5-year refresh → 250/5 = 50 kgCO2e/year per laptop
  • difference × 1,500 laptops ≈ 50 tCO2e/year saved just by extending the cycle

That's the argument that convinces the CFO (the 5-year refresh also costs less). The full policy is generated from references/device-lifecycle-policy.md: ask Claude "generate the device lifecycle policy for our fleet" and answer the block questions (procurement, repair-first, reuse cascade, WEEE).

The skill's golden rule — GreenOps rides FinOps: the carbon column joins the FinOps review meeting that already exists; a cost anomaly and a carbon anomaly are usually the same anomaly.

And the "carbon neutral" claim?

It must be removed or reworded by 27 September 2026 (EmpCo). The skill suggests a substantiable rewording, e.g.: "PUE 1.41 and improving, 35% certified renewable energy, checkout SCI published and decreasing" — measured data, declared methodology, zero banned words.

Recap: what to ask, in order

  1. "What sustainability obligations does our IT have?" → maturity + obligation matrix
  2. "Let's prepare the data centre EED report" → checklist and gaps
  3. "Calculate the SCI of our service X" → script with your numbers
  4. "What should I ask our cloud provider?" → Module F questionnaire
  5. "Generate our device lifecycle policy" → policy ready for review
  6. "Which KPIs do I bring to the board?" → governance set

The three most common beginner mistakes

  1. Asking for "everything at once" — skills work in steps. Answer one question at a time; you can always say "I don't know" and the skill will tell you how to estimate or where to find the data.
  2. Treating outputs as final — tables, matrices, and estimates are working drafts with explicit sources and assumptions. Verify them before they go into an official document.
  3. Using the wrong level of skill — this plugin works at the management/compliance level. For technical code audits (green coding patterns, runtime measurements, mobile) use the companion sustainable-code plugin (the gc-* skills): this plugin stops where the IDE begins.
